# NTU sensor voltage test — 10 October 2026

## Findings

The DFRobot reference equation produced approximately 1,242–2,019 NTU in
three captured sessions. These are computed estimates, not verified turbidity
measurements. The generic purchased module has not been identified as DFRobot
SEN0189, and no certified NTU standards or reference turbidimeter were used.
The conversion is not accurate enough to use as a calibrated NTU reading for
this setup. Its numerical error cannot yet be quantified because actual sample
turbidity is unknown.

## Hardware and conversion

ESP32 was powered by laptop USB. Sensor V and ADC V used the verified VIN/5V
rail; all grounds were common and ADC ADDR was grounded (0x48). Sensor A
connected directly to A0, without a voltage divider. A bidirectional I2C level
converter connected SDA/SCL to GPIO21/22: HV=5V, LV=3.3V. Firmware assumes
ADS1115, GAIN_TWOTHIRDS, 128 samples/s, with 64 readings per reported voltage.
The actual ADC chip identity still needs confirmation; shared PCB labels are
not sufficient. Verify reported voltage against a multimeter.

The DFRobot reference conversion is:

```text
NTU_estimate = -1120.4 * V^2 + 5742.3 * V - 4352.9
```

V is sensor analog output voltage in volts. The dashboard uses this equation
only within the approximate plotted range 2.5–4.2V. Outside that range it shows
“Outside curve range”, rather than extrapolating. Mean NTU estimate is the mean
of the individual converted voltage windows, not conversion of the overall
mean voltage; the quadratic makes those quantities slightly different.

## Why these estimates cannot be trusted as actual NTU

DFRobot describes its curve as approximate and warns that actual measurements
can differ. Its manufacturer FAQ states that this sensor is primarily qualitative
and does not recommend conversion to standard NTU for accurate quantitative use.
The purchased sensor is a generic module, so even the reference curve's
applicability is unverified. Visually clear water does not supply a known NTU
value, and a stable voltage is evidence of repeatability, not calibration accuracy.

The friend-model comparison also does not establish actual NTU. It uses:

```text
NTU_friend = clamp(1 + 5783 * ln(3.22 / V), 0, 3000)
```

It returned 0.0 NTU for the screenshot voltages because they exceed the assumed
3.22V clear-water reference and the resulting negative estimates are clamped.
This is a model artifact, not proof of zero turbidity. Its baseline and borrowed
sensitivity have not been calibrated for this module.

## Next calibration step

1. Obtain certified turbidity reference solutions with documented NTU values,
   tolerance, expiry and handling instructions, or have samples measured using
   a calibrated reference turbidimeter. Ordinary water with an assumed label
   is not a verified standard. Follow the standards' preparation instructions.
2. Confirm the ADC model, sensor supply and measured voltage scaling. Keep
   container, probe position, depth, temperature and lighting consistent; clean
   the optical window and remove bubbles. Keep the probe's non-waterproof parts dry.
3. Measure multiple standards spanning the intended range, including standards
   near the project's low-NTU thresholds. Record repeat runs, voltage spread,
   settling time and drift, returning to a reference between sessions.
4. Fit a module-specific voltage-to-NTU equation from those labeled measurements.
   Compare appropriate monotonic models; do not assume the DFRobot quadratic
   or logarithmic model is correct. Reserve independent standards for validation.
5. Report validation error, repeatability and the usable range. Do not extrapolate
   outside the calibrated range. If low-NTU changes cannot be resolved reliably,
   use a more suitable sensor instead of claiming accuracy from a fitted equation.
